APEX FINANCIAL SERVICES, INC.
INFORMATION SECURITY AND DATA GOVERNANCE POLICY

Policy Number: IT-012
Version: 2.5
Effective Date: January 1, 2025
Last Reviewed: December 15, 2024
Owner: Chief Information Security Officer
Approved By: Board of Directors

1. PURPOSE AND SCOPE

This policy establishes the Firm's information security and data governance framework to protect confidential, proprietary, and customer information. It applies to all systems, applications, data, employees, contractors, and third-party service providers of Apex Financial Services, Inc.

This policy supports compliance with the SEC Safeguards Rule (Regulation S-P), the SEC Identity Theft Red Flags Rule (Regulation S-ID), the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act (GLBA), and FINRA rules governing cybersecurity practices.

2. DATA CLASSIFICATION

2.1 Classification Levels
All data created, collected, or processed by the Firm must be classified into one of four categories:

Restricted: Data whose unauthorized disclosure could cause severe harm to the Firm, its customers, or third parties. Includes personally identifiable information (PII), authentication credentials, financial account data, trading strategies, and material non-public information (MNPI).

Confidential: Data intended for internal use only whose unauthorized disclosure could harm the Firm. Includes employee records, internal financial reports, vendor contracts, and compliance reports.

Internal: Data for general internal use with limited risk if disclosed. Includes organizational charts, general policies, and operational procedures.

Public: Data approved for external disclosure with no expected harm from dissemination.

2.2 Handling Requirements
Restricted data must be encrypted at rest and in transit using AES-256 or equivalent. Access to Restricted data must be controlled on a strict need-to-know basis and logged. Confidential data must be protected from unauthorized external disclosure.

3. ACCESS CONTROL

3.1 Principle of Least Privilege
All access to Firm systems and data must follow the principle of least privilege. Employees must be granted the minimum level of access necessary to perform their job functions.

3.2 User Authentication
All users must authenticate using multi-factor authentication (MFA) to access Firm systems containing Restricted or Confidential data. Single-factor authentication is not permitted for such systems after March 31, 2025.

3.3 Privileged Access Management
Privileged accounts (system administrators, database administrators, network engineers) must be managed through the Firm's Privileged Access Management (PAM) system. Privileged sessions must be recorded and logged.

3.4 Access Review
All user access rights must be reviewed at minimum quarterly. Access for terminated employees must be revoked within four hours of termination notification.

4. CLOUD COMPUTING GOVERNANCE

4.1 Approved Cloud Providers
The Firm may use cloud services only from providers approved by the Technology Governance Committee. Approval requires the provider to demonstrate SOC 2 Type II compliance, ISO 27001 certification, FedRAMP authorization (for government-regulated data), and ability to meet the Firm's data residency requirements.

4.2 Data Residency
Customer financial data and regulatory records must be stored in data centers located within the United States unless an exception is approved by the Chief Compliance Officer and Chief Information Security Officer.

4.3 Cloud Vendor Contracts
Contracts with cloud service providers must include provisions addressing data ownership (all data belongs to the Firm), right to audit, notification of security incidents within 24 hours, data portability upon contract termination, and compliance with applicable regulations.

4.4 Shared Responsibility Model
The Firm and cloud providers operate under a shared responsibility model. The Firm retains full responsibility for data classification, access control configurations, application-level security, and compliance with regulatory requirements regardless of the underlying infrastructure provider.

5. CYBERSECURITY INCIDENT RESPONSE

5.1 Incident Classification
Security incidents are classified as follows:

Critical: Unauthorized access to or exfiltration of Restricted data; ransomware or destructive malware; confirmed breach of a major system. Requires immediate response and regulatory notification assessment.

High: Suspected unauthorized access; distributed denial-of-service (DDoS) attack; compromise of privileged credentials.

Medium: Detected malware that has not accessed sensitive data; policy violations by employees; phishing attempts.

Low: Minor policy violations; isolated malware with no data access; unsuccessful intrusion attempts.

5.2 Response Timeline
Critical incidents must be reported to the CISO within one hour and to the Chief Compliance Officer within four hours. High incidents must be reported to the CISO within four hours.

5.3 Regulatory Notification
In the event of a security breach affecting customer non-public personal information (NPI), the Firm must assess its notification obligations under Regulation S-P, applicable state data breach notification laws, and FINRA Rule 4370. Customer notification, where required, must occur within the timeframes required by applicable law, which may be as short as 30 days.

5.4 Incident Recordkeeping
All security incidents must be documented in the Firm's incident tracking system, regardless of classification. Documentation must include the date of detection, nature of the incident, systems affected, remediation steps taken, and lessons learned. Incident records must be retained for a minimum of six years.

6. VENDOR AND THIRD-PARTY RISK MANAGEMENT

6.1 Due Diligence
All third-party vendors with access to Firm data or systems must undergo information security due diligence prior to engagement. Due diligence must include review of the vendor's security policies, certifications (e.g., SOC 2, ISO 27001), penetration testing history, and incident response capabilities.

6.2 Ongoing Monitoring
Third-party vendors must be subject to annual security assessments. Critical vendors (those with access to Restricted data) must also be subject to continuous monitoring using the Firm's vendor risk management platform.

6.3 Contractual Requirements
All vendor contracts must include information security provisions addressing data protection requirements, incident notification (within 24 hours of discovery of a breach affecting Firm data), right to audit, compliance with applicable regulations, and data return or destruction upon contract termination.

7. ENCRYPTION STANDARDS

7.1 Data in Transit
All data classified as Restricted or Confidential must be encrypted during transmission using TLS 1.2 or higher. The use of SSL or TLS 1.0 and 1.1 is prohibited.

7.2 Data at Rest
All Restricted data must be encrypted at rest using AES-256 or an equivalent NIST-approved algorithm. Encryption keys must be managed through the Firm's enterprise key management system.

7.3 Email Encryption
Emails containing Restricted data must be encrypted using the Firm's approved secure email gateway. Employees must not send Restricted data via unencrypted email.

8. MOBILE DEVICE MANAGEMENT

8.1 BYOD Policy
Employees who use personal devices (Bring Your Own Device or BYOD) for Firm business must enroll the device in the Firm's Mobile Device Management (MDM) system. Firm data on personal devices is subject to remote wipe if the device is lost, stolen, or if the employee separates from the Firm.

8.2 Approved Applications
Only applications approved by IT Security may be used to access Firm data on mobile devices. The use of personal cloud storage applications (e.g., personal Dropbox, personal Google Drive) to store Firm data is prohibited.

9. PENETRATION TESTING AND VULNERABILITY MANAGEMENT

9.1 Penetration Testing
The Firm must conduct external penetration tests at minimum annually and following any significant change to its technology infrastructure. Penetration tests must be conducted by qualified third-party security firms.

9.2 Vulnerability Scanning
Internal and external vulnerability scans must be conducted at minimum monthly. Critical and High vulnerabilities must be remediated within 30 days of discovery. Medium vulnerabilities must be remediated within 90 days.

10. EMPLOYEE TRAINING AND AWARENESS

All employees must complete information security awareness training annually, including phishing simulation exercises. Employees with access to Restricted data must complete role-specific security training covering data handling, social engineering, and incident reporting procedures.

11. POLICY COMPLIANCE AND ENFORCEMENT

Violations of this policy may result in disciplinary action up to and including termination of employment and referral to law enforcement. The CISO is responsible for monitoring compliance with this policy and reporting material violations to the Chief Compliance Officer and the Board's Risk Committee.
